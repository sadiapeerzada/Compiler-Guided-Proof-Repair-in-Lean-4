Compiler-Guided Repair for
Natural-Language-to-Lean Formalization
Sadia Peerzada
sadiapeerzada2006@gmail.com
Abstract. We execute a paired local-model study of one-shot proof gen-
eration (A), blind repair (B), and compiler-guided repair (C) for natural-
language statements paired with ﬁxed Lean theorem types. B and C
reuse exactly the same failed initial candidate and permit at most three
repairs each. The frozen dataset contains 60 test statements and 15 de-
velopment statements across ﬁve categories, screened under an explic-
itly AI-assisted semantic policy. Using one hash-pinned 4-bit Qwen2.5-
7B instruction model, the observed veriﬁed success counts are A: 0/60
(0.0%), B: 1/60 (1.7%), C: 1/60 (1.7%). The C–B diﬀerence is +0.0 per-
centage points; the 95% family-cluster bootstrap interval is [+0.0, +0.0]
points. The recorded experiment does not establish a robust advantage
for compiler feedback; a non-signiﬁcant or non-informative test is not
evidence of equivalence. We preserve raw model responses, compiler di-
agnostics, token streams, stopping events and checkpoints. The evidence
concerns ﬁxed-type proof synthesis, not unrestricted generation of faith-
ful formal statements. Single-seed inference, related theorem families,
author-assisted AI adjudication, and library/pretraining exposure limit
generalization.
Keywords: Lean 4 · compiler feedback · proof repair · local inference
· semantic review
1 Introduction and Research Questions
An informal mathematical statement can be paired with a formal theorem type
yet still lack a proof accepted by a theorem prover. A generated proof may fail
because of syntax, unavailable names, type mismatches, an incomplete tactic
argument, or a mathematical mistake. A compiler supplies concrete evidence
about these failures, but additional generation alone may also repair them. A
useful comparison must distinguish diagnostic feedback from the beneﬁt of more
calls.
Our primary question is whether compiler-guided repair improves veriﬁed
success over equally budgeted blind repair, conditioned on the same initial can-
didate. Secondary questions compare each repair condition with one-shot gen-
eration and describe outcomes by initial error category. We do not assume that
feedback helps, or interpret a failure to reject as equivalence. The experimental
unit is a theorem trajectory, not an individual repair attempt.

2 Related Work
Lean 4 provides a dependently typed language and kernel-checked proof infras-
tructure [4]; Mathlib supplies the mathematical library used here [6]. Bench-
marks such as miniF2F [8] support formal theorem-proving evaluation, while
ProofNet studies paired informal and formal mathematics [1]. LeanDojo studies
tool-supported theorem proving and retrieval [7]. These settings motivate careful
separation of problem statements, proof generation, library access and veriﬁca-
tion. Our experiment is a bounded paired comparison using a general local in-
struction model. It includes no retrieval service, proof search tree, ﬁne-tuning, or
paid hosted inference, and should not be compared directly with systems using
diﬀerent tasks, data or compute budgets.
3 System Architecture and Veriﬁcation
The pipeline loads immutable English/type pairs, renders an initial prompt, gen-
erates one candidate, extracts the requested Lean block, and veriﬁes it against
the ﬁxed reference type. A and both repair branches share the exact initial
bytes and SHA-256. Each repair uses a fresh model conversation and indepen-
dent context-derived random stream. B receives the theorem, its current can-
didate and ﬁxed blind-feedback text. C receives the same template structure
with structured diagnostics from its immediately preceding failed compilation.
Neither branch receives the other branch’s candidates or conversation.
The strict veriﬁer rejects placeholder proofs and unauthorized declarations,
checks the exact elaborated theorem type, and inspects target axiom dependen-
cies. Only the standard propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound depen-
dencies are accepted. A reference placeholder is used only to compare types in a
separate namespace; it is never counted as a target proof, and a target depending
on sorryAx is rejected. Compiler JSON, source text, exit status, failure kind and
elapsed time are stored. Compilation proves neither the English interpretation
nor the quality of explanatory prose.
Source-token and declaration guards run before compilation; axiom-policy
rejection can also occur afterward. Such events are not relabeled as compiler er-
rors. C stops if genuine candidate diagnostics are unavailable; it is never supplied
an invented compiler message. Timeouts, context-limit failures and interrupted
calls remain explicit missing/incomplete outcomes.

4 Dataset, Provenance and Semantic Audit
Starting from 96 prepared candidates, we exclude one exact duplicate and 20
nonduplicate records lacking adequate item-level provenance. The resulting
75 records comprise 16 textbook-derived statements [2] and 59 adaptations
of pinned formal-library statements. Citations, original source bytes, license
notices and item-level attribution are retained. Textbook adaptations retain
CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution; formal-library material retains Apache-2.0 notices.
These are evidence-based AI-assisted source assessments, not invented publisher
permissions or independent legal clearance.
Two original records were corrected: a positive-modulus premise was restored,
and ambiguous summation endpoints were made explicit. Both proofs were rever-
iﬁed and both pairs were reviewed again with a separate Phi-4 model. They re-
main excluded for source-provenance reasons; corrections were not used to inﬂate
eligible counts. Original records and before/after histories remain available.
The approved replacement policy uses explicit AI adjudication of quantiﬁers,
domains, assumptions, strength, endpoints and equivalent encodings. Phi-3.5
and Phi-4 audit responses are retained, including schema failures and unsup-
ported objections. The authoring coding assistant adjudicated the exact selected
pairs using candidate-speciﬁc mathematical comparisons and reference evidence.
It is not author-independent human review. An uncertain or unfaithful ﬁnal ad-
judication is inadmissible; unreliable raw AI output is not a faithful vote.
The independent reviewer models were demonstrably fallible: for example,
one claimed that 1(1 + 1) was odd. Exact arithmetic refutes that alleged coun-
terexample. Generic placeholders, hallucinated hypotheses and proof-method
confusion were separately ﬂagged. All 96 original reference types have strict
Lean evidence; handwritten bounded probes cover 26,467 accepted ﬁnite assign-
ments, with 88 exact predicates and eight approximate numerical probes. These
checks supplement, rather than establish, English equivalence.

5 Frozen Experimental Design
The primary model is mlx-community/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-4bit, run oﬄine
with MLX [5, 3]. Its immutable converted snapshot is c26a38f6a37d0a51b4e9
a1eb3026530fa35d9fed . Inventory hashes bind all weights, tokenizer ﬁles and
the native chat template. Quantization is 4-bit with group size 64. MLX 0.32.4,
MLX-LM 0.32.2 and Transformers 5.21.0 are recorded. Decoding uses tempera-
ture 0.2, top- p 1, at most 2,000 output tokens and an 8,192-token total context
cap. One primary seed, 2027, is used; three-seed robustness is not claimed. The
local host uses an Apple M4 processor with 16 GiB physical memory.
The per-call seed is the ﬁrst unsigned 32-bit word of SHA-256 of the base seed,
phase/problem/condition/step context and exact prompt hash. Consequently,
B’s stopping behavior does not shift C’s random stream. All conditions use
the same model and environment. A has zero repairs; B and C each permit
up to three, with at most seven logical generations per problem including the
shared initial call. Success stops a branch. Model weights are released during
the memory-heavy Lean check and reloaded from the same veriﬁed bytes on the
next call. This preserves independent KV state and incurs measurable loading
overhead.
Exploratory development pilots exposed Lean 3-style syntax and high res-
ident memory cost. Before any held-out generation, the ﬁnal protocol added
the same explicit Lean 4 syntax instruction to all three conditions and ﬁxed
the memory schedule. This was syntax/implementation development, not test-
outcome tuning; the format diagnostic still failed mathematical veriﬁcation. Pro-
totypes are preserved and excluded from study estimates. The ﬁnal three-case
pilot passed the provenance/control audit, and an actual resume invocation gen-
erated no new calls. No model, prompt, dataset or budget changed after held-out
execution began.
Conditions execute B before C rather than in randomized order. Each call has
a durable intent, raw response and token stream; each veriﬁcation and branch
transition has a checkpoint. Resume reuses completed outputs. An incomplete
generation intent consumes its logical call and is not silently regenerated. The
environment is Lean 4.32.0 with clean Mathlib commit 81a5d257c8e410db227a
6665ed08f64fea08e997. There is no provider fallback or paid inference path.

7 Compiler Errors and Qualitative Analysis
Table 3 groups failed initial candidates into a single primary category using
a ﬁxed message taxonomy; Figure 2 shows veriﬁed repair outcomes. Parser,
unknown-name, type/elaboration and tactic/goal failures are distinguished.
Safety guards and infrastructure failures are separate from compiler errors. The
artifact also records all attempted-candidate error events; those repeated events
are not independent problem observations. As a post-hoc process diagnostic, 90
of 178 completed B repairs and 70 of 172 completed C repairs were byte-identical
to the preceding candidate. These counts describe repetition, not additional
tests or independent samples.

8 Semantic Faithfulness and Threats to Validity
Successful proofs must have the exact reviewed reference type; changing a quan-
tiﬁer, adding a hypothesis or weakening the conclusion cannot substitute for
solving that target. Nevertheless, faithfulness to English remains conditional on
the AI-adjudicated reference. We report review coverage and provenance, not
independently measured semantic accuracy. No AI output is represented as an
independent human judgment.
The authoring/adjudicating assistant can share systematic biases with
the dataset construction process. Separate Phi reviewers were unreliable, and
bounded mathematical probes are not exhaustive proofs of informal meaning.
Source/library exposure and direct named-lemma reuse may aﬀect performance;
the dataset is not a contamination-free benchmark. Curated family boundaries
are mathematical judgments, not guarantees of statistical independence. The
development set has only ﬁve family clusters, and results use one model, one
quantization, one primary seed and one local hardware setting. The model’s
knowledge can predate the pinned Lean/Mathlib interfaces. Fixed call and
context caps can stop repair without exhausting mathematical possibilities.
These limits preclude broad claims about compiler feedback, larger models or
unrestricted NL-to-formal translation.

10 Conclusion
This study records a controlled local comparison of one-shot, blind and compiler-
guided proof repair for 60 ﬁxed theorem targets with shared initial candidates.
The recorded experiment does not establish a robust advantage for compiler
feedback; a non-signiﬁcant or non-informative test is not evidence of equiva-
lence. The contribution is the reproducible paired evidence and its failure anal-
ysis under a clearly delimited low-resource conﬁguration, not a claim of general
superiority, independent human semantic validation, or complete unconstrained
natural-language formalization.
